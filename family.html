Parents & return

Looking after family in UP — and coming home

The hardest NRI problems aren't paperwork. They're a parent who falls at night, or a move back that costs more tax than it should. Plan both.

Care for parents from a distance

  • Save the national emergency number 112 in your parents' phones and yours; it connects police, fire and ambulance services.
  • Map the nearest 24-hour hospital with an ICU, and keep the route and a taxi contact written by the phone.
  • Health cover: since late 2024, every Indian aged 70 and above can get Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY cover through the Ayushman Vay Vandana card, regardless of income. Combine it with private insurance where possible.
  • Pensioners must submit an annual life certificate; Jeevan Pramaan lets them do it digitally.
  • A local person you trust — a relative, neighbour or paid care manager — who visits weekly and has keys, medicines list and doctor's number.

Professional elder-care companies now operate in UP's larger cities with monthly plans covering check-ins, doctor visits and emergency coordination. Ask what is included at night, which hospitals they work with, and who actually turns up.

Moving back to UP: get the order right

  1. Time your return. Many returning NRIs qualify as RNOR (resident but not ordinarily resident) for a period, which keeps most foreign income out of Indian tax. Check your status.
  2. Re-designate accounts. NRE and NRO accounts must be converted to resident accounts on return; FCNR deposits can run to maturity and foreign currency can be kept in an RFC account.
  3. Tell everyone your new status — banks, brokers, mutual funds and your foreign pension providers.
  4. Health insurance first, before you board: pre-existing-condition waiting periods start from the policy date.
  5. Schools and admissions run on fixed calendars; start a year ahead.
Coming back with foreign retirement savings or shares? Get advice before you move — timing can change the tax on them.
