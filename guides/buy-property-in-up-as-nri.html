1. What you can and can't buy

Under India's foreign-exchange rules, NRIs and Overseas Citizens of India can buy any number of residential or commercial properties without asking the Reserve Bank of India. What they cannot buy is agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse. They can inherit those, and they can receive residential or commercial property as a gift from a relative.

In UP this matters more than in most states, because so much land around growing towns is still recorded as agricultural. A plot sold as “residential” may still be agricultural in the khatauni. If it is, you can't buy it as an NRI, and a resident buying it may need a change of land use first.

2. Checks before you pay

  • Land record: look up the khata on UP Bhulekh. The seller's name should be recorded, with no stay orders or other names you weren't told about.
  • Encumbrances: run a property search on IGRS UP for earlier sales, mortgages or gifts of the same land.
  • Title chain: a lawyer should trace ownership back about 30 years and match each link to a registered document and mutation.
  • Project registration: for flats and plotted projects, search UP RERA for the project, the promoter and any complaints.
  • Authority papers: in Noida, Greater Noida, the Yamuna Expressway area or Lucknow, much land is leasehold from a development authority. Check the lease, any transfer charges and dues.
  • On the ground: someone should visit, confirm the boundaries match the map and ask the neighbours who is in possession.

3. Paying the right way

Pay only through banking channels: money remitted from abroad, or from your NRE, FCNR(B) or NRO accounts. Never pay cash, and never pay into an individual's account when the seller is a company or authority. Keep every bank advice. If you ever want to send the sale proceeds back abroad, this payment trail is what makes repatriation possible.

Under-the-table “cash components” are common and dangerous: they understate the price, expose you to tax trouble and leave you with no proof of what you paid.

4. Stamp duty and the registry

UP charges the same stamp duty to NRIs and residents. Duty is calculated on the higher of the agreed price and the circle-rate value. As widely reported for 2026: 7% for a man, 6% for a woman on property up to ₹1 crore, around 6.5% for a joint woman-and-man purchase, plus a 1% registration fee. On a ₹60 lakh flat that is ₹4.8 lakh for a man and ₹4.2 lakh for a woman. Run your own numbers.

Duty is paid by e-stamp and the registry appointment is booked through IGRS UP. You'll need PAN, passport (and OCI card if you have one), photos and two witnesses. If you can't be there, a specific power of attorney holder can sign for you — see our POA guide.

5. After the registry

  1. Apply for mutation so the khatauni or the authority's record shows your name. Registration does not do this automatically.
  2. Transfer the utilities and house tax into your name.
  3. Store certified copies of the deed and the updated record somewhere outside India as well.
  4. Diary a check of the khatauni every six months.

Selling later? The buyer will have to withhold tax on your sale price. Read NRI property sale and TDS in 2026 before you list.
