A short history

After slavery ended in the British Empire, plantation colonies turned to indentured labour from India. Mauritius took the first Indians in 1834, British Guiana in 1838 and Trinidad and Jamaica in 1845; Fiji followed from 1879. By the time the system ended in 1916, well over a million people had sailed. Early recruits came mostly from Bihar and the Chota Nagpur plateau; from the 1870s the eastern districts of what is now Uttar Pradesh became the main source. For Fiji, historian Brij V. Lal found that UP supplied about 80% of North Indian emigrants from the 1880s onwards.

Step 1: gather what the family already knows

Write down every name, place, ship, date, caste name, song and story, however garbled. Look for documents with writing in Hindi, Urdu or the old Kaithi script. A grandmother's “Fyzabad” or “Bustee” is a clue.

Step 2: find the emigration pass

Emigrants were registered at the depot before sailing. The pass and the immigrant registers in the receiving colony usually record: name, father's name, age, sex, caste, village, thana and zila, the ship, and a depot or immigrant number. Where to look:

  • Mauritius: the Indian immigration archives at the Mahatma Gandhi Institute; the Aapravasi Ghat site interprets the history.
  • Fiji: the National Archives of Fiji hold emigration passes.
  • Suriname: registers of Hindostani contract labourers held by the Nationaal Archief in the Netherlands and Suriname's national archives.
  • Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana, South Africa: the national or provincial archives, which hold ship lists and registers.

Step 3: translate colonial spelling into today's map

Clerks wrote what they heard. District names have also changed: Faizabad is now Ayodhya district, and several new districts have been carved out of old ones — Sant Kabir Nagar and Siddharthnagar from Basti, Ambedkar Nagar from Faizabad, Shravasti and Balrampur from Bahraich and Gonda. Work from the thana (police station) named on the pass: villages are found far more reliably by thana than by district alone. Our district finder lists all 75 districts with their divisions.

Step 4: confirm locally

Once you have a candidate village, check it against the khatauni on UP Bhulekh and, with local help, the family (parivar) register. Elders' memories of who “went to Mirich” (Mauritius) or “Chini Tapu” (Trinidad) can still survive in some villages.

Step 5: visit with care

Go with a guide who speaks Awadhi or Bhojpuri, and with modest expectations. Sometimes a family is found; sometimes only the land, the pond and the temple. Many descendants say that is enough.
