Your property in UP, protected from abroad

Land and houses left behind are where most NRI problems start: a name never mutated, a tax bill unpaid, a cousin who “just built a wall.” Here is how the UP system works and what to do.

The records that prove what you own

In Uttar Pradesh, ownership rests on a stack of documents, and you need every layer to be in your name and consistent.

  • Registered title document — the sale deed, gift deed, partition deed or probated will, registered at the sub-registrar. Registration and stamp duty run through IGRS UP.
  • Khatauni — the record of rights for land, listing the holders of each khata. You can view the current khatauni on UP Bhulekh by district, tehsil and village.
  • Khasra / plot number and map — identifies the exact parcel. A khatauni can be right while the boundary on the ground is wrong.
  • Mutation (dakhil-kharij) order — the revenue order that moves the name in the record after a sale or death. Registration alone does not update the khatauni.
  • Authority papers for urban plots — allotment letter, lease deed and transfer memorandum from bodies such as the Noida, Greater Noida, YEIDA or Lucknow development authorities, plus municipal house-tax records.
Check twice a year. Pull your khatauni from UP Bhulekh every six months and save a PDF. A changed entry you catch early is a correction; one you catch after five years is a lawsuit.

What NRIs and OCIs can and can't buy

Under India's foreign-exchange rules, NRIs and OCI cardholders can buy residential and commercial property without RBI approval and with no limit on the number of properties. They cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or farmhouses, but they can inherit them and can sell inherited farmland to a resident Indian citizen. Payment must come through banking channels: inward remittance or NRE, FCNR(B) or NRO accounts.

Protecting property you don't live in

  1. Keep every bill in your name and paid — house tax, water, electricity. Paid receipts are everyday evidence of possession.
  2. Fence, sign and photograph. Ask someone you trust for dated, geo-tagged photos each quarter.
  3. Use a specific power of attorney, never a general one. Limit it to named acts and revoke it when the job is done.
  4. Register rent agreements and use short terms; never let a tenant pay tax or bills in their own name.
  5. Finish mutation after every death or sale — unmutated land is the easiest to grab.

When something goes wrong

Encroachment and fraud usually need three tracks at once: a written complaint to the tehsil or SDM with your records, a police complaint where there is trespass or forgery, and a civil suit for an injunction if construction has started. UP's online grievance system (Jansunwai) and the UP NRI Department both accept grievances from NRIs. Act fast: delay helps the person in possession.
