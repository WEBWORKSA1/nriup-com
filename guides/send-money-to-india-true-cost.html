Where the money goes

Every transfer has two costs: the visible fee, and the exchange-rate margin — the gap between the rate you get and the mid-market rate. Providers that advertise “zero fees” still earn on the margin. The only fair comparison is how many rupees arrive for the same amount sent.

A worked example

Say you send $2,000. At a mid-market rate of ₹92.00 your family would get ₹1,84,000. A provider offering ₹91.10 with no fee delivers ₹1,82,200. The “free” transfer cost ₹1,800, or about 0.98%. Do that every month and a 1% leak costs roughly $240 a year. (Rates here are illustrative — check today's live rate in our calculator.)

Five habits that save the most

  1. Compare two quotes on the day you send, measured against the mid-market rate.
  2. Watch the promo cliff. First-transfer rates often end after one transfer or a capped amount.
  3. Batch when fees are fixed. One larger transfer beats four small ones.
  4. Send to the right account. Savings from abroad belong in NRE, where interest is tax-free in India while you're an NRI. Pick your accounts.
  5. Mind your bank's own margin. Some Indian banks apply a conversion spread on incoming foreign currency; ask yours.

Large amounts

For property purchases or big gifts, ask providers for a large-transfer rate and confirm the purpose code your Indian bank will record. Keep the bank advice (FIRC or equivalent) — it's your proof that the money came from abroad, which matters if you ever want to repatriate it.

Never send money to an “RBI”, “customs” or “tax” official who contacts you to release a prize, parcel or refund. These are scams.
