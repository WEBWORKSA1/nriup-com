Special, not general

A general power of attorney lets the holder do almost anything with your property. A special (specific) power of attorney names one property and lists the exact acts allowed — for example, “to apply for mutation of khata no. ___ in village ___ and to collect the order.” For NRIs the rule is simple: use special POAs, one job at a time, and choose the attorney as carefully as you'd choose a co-owner.

Signing it abroad

  1. Draft in India-ready form. Ask the lawyer or registry that will use it for their preferred wording; registries in UP can be particular.
  2. Execute it properly. Either sign before an officer at the Indian embassy or consulate, or sign before a local notary and get an apostille if you live in a Hague Convention country.
  3. Attach copies of your passport (and OCI card), the attorney's ID and photos of both of you.
  4. Courier the original to India.

Stamping it in UP

A POA signed outside India must be stamped in India within three months of first being received here. In practice that means presenting it to the stamp authorities in the district for adjudication and paying the duty that applies. Miss the deadline and you face penalties, and the document may be refused when it matters.

Does it need to be registered?

It depends on what it will be used for. For anything involving transfer of property, sub-registrars generally insist on a properly authenticated and, often, registered POA. Ask the sub-registrar's office (or your lawyer) before your attorney turns up to sign a deed.

Safety rules

  • Give the POA an end date or tie it to a single transaction.
  • Have sale money paid directly into your own account, never the attorney's.
  • Keep a copy of every document the attorney signs.
  • Revoke it in writing when the job is done, register the revocation where the POA was registered, and tell anyone who relied on it.
Many property frauds against NRIs use a POA the owner never signed. If you learn of one, file a police complaint for forgery and alert the sub-registrar at once.
