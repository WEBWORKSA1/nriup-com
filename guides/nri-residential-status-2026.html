Which law applies to which year

The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026, replacing the 1961 Act and replacing the old “previous year / assessment year” pair with a single tax year. Your status for tax year 2026-27 onwards is decided under section 6 of the 2025 Act. Your status for 2025-26 and earlier is still decided under section 6 of the 1961 Act, even if the assessment happens later.

Step 1: are you resident at all?

You are resident in a tax year if either:

  • you were in India for 182 days or more in that year; or
  • you were in India for 60 days or more in that year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years.

The 60-day test is modified for Indians abroad:

  • An Indian citizen who leaves India in the year for employment abroad (or as ship crew): the 60 days becomes 182.
  • An Indian citizen or person of Indian origin visiting India: the 60 days becomes 182 — unless their Indian income (excluding foreign sources) exceeds ₹15 lakh, in which case it becomes 120 days. Someone caught by the 120-day rule is resident but not ordinarily resident.

Step 2: the deemed-resident rule

An Indian citizen whose Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh and who is not liable to tax in any other country by reason of residence or domicile is treated as resident regardless of days — again as not ordinarily resident. This is aimed at people living in countries without personal income tax, which is why Gulf-based NRIs with large Indian rental or business income should check it every year.

Step 3: ordinarily resident or not?

A resident is “not ordinarily resident” (RNOR) if they were non-resident in 9 of the 10 previous years, or were in India for 729 days or less in the previous 7 years — plus anyone made resident only by the 120-day or deemed-residency rules. RNOR status is valuable for returning NRIs because most foreign income stays outside Indian tax.

Practical habits

  • Count days from passport stamps or travel records, not memory. Arrival and departure days need care.
  • Keep a Tax Residency Certificate from your country of residence if you claim treaty benefits.
  • Update your status with banks, brokers and fund houses when it changes.
