The rule in one paragraph

When a non-resident sells property in India, the buyer must deduct tax at source from the payment. For long-term capital gains — property held more than 24 months — the rate is 12.5% (for transfers on or after 23 July 2024, without indexation), plus surcharge and 4% cess. For short-term gains, deduction is at the seller's slab rates, which buyers usually apply at 30%. There is no ₹50 lakh threshold as there is for resident sellers: it applies from the first rupee. From tax year 2026-27 the provision is section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025; it used to be section 195.

Why the number is so big

Unless the seller produces a certificate from the tax department, the buyer computes the deduction on the whole sale consideration. The surcharge depends on the amount: 10% above ₹50 lakh and 15% above ₹1 crore (capped at 15% for these gains). So the effective rates are 13% up to ₹50 lakh, 14.3% between ₹50 lakh and ₹1 crore, and 14.95% above ₹1 crore.

The fix: a lower or nil deduction certificate

The seller can apply to the Income Tax Department for a certificate authorising the buyer to deduct at a lower rate, or not at all, based on the estimated tax actually payable. Under the 2025 Act the application form is Form 128 (it was Form 13 under the 1961 Act). Apply as soon as you have a buyer and a price: processing takes time and the certificate is issued for a specific buyer and transaction. You'll need your PAN, the purchase deed, proof of cost and improvements, and the sale agreement or draft.

What changed for buyers on 1 October 2026

Until 30 September 2026, a resident buyer had to obtain a TAN (tax deduction account number) just to deduct tax on an NRI purchase — a real deterrent for ordinary home buyers. From 1 October 2026, resident individuals and HUFs can instead deposit and report the deduction using their PAN, through Schedule E of Form 141, a challan-cum-statement. The obligation to deduct remains; only the paperwork is lighter. Tell your buyer: it makes your property easier to sell.

Exemptions and getting the money out

  • Reinvesting the gain in another residential house in India, or in specified bonds within the time limits, can reduce or remove the tax. These were sections 54 and 54EC under the old Act.
  • Sale proceeds land in your NRO account. You can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO balances with a CA's certificate and the prescribed forms. Proceeds of up to two residential properties bought with foreign exchange can be repatriated within the original amount paid.
  • Any excess TDS comes back only after you file an Indian return — so file, even if you have no other Indian income.
