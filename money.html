Money & tax hub

Money between home and UP, without leaks

India received $135.46 billion in remittances in FY25, more than any country on earth. Here's how to make sure less of yours disappears in margins, wrong accounts and avoidable tax.

1. Put money in the right account

Once you become a non-resident, your ordinary Indian savings accounts must be redesignated as NRO. Money you earn abroad belongs in an NRE account (fully repatriable, interest tax-free in India while you're an NRI). Savings you want to keep in dollars, pounds or euros can sit in an FCNR(B) deposit. Income you earn in India — rent, pension, dividends, sale proceeds — goes into NRO, where interest is taxed and repatriation is capped at USD 1 million per financial year with paperwork. Use the account picker.

2. Measure the real cost of every transfer

Providers earn mainly on the exchange rate. A 1% gap on a $2,000 monthly transfer costs about $240 a year — before fees. Compare quotes against the mid-market rate on the day you send with our true-cost calculator. The US and the UAE are India's two largest remittance sources: RBI's latest survey put their shares at 27.7% and 19.2% in FY24.

3. Know your tax status every year

India's new Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026, replacing the 1961 Act. The residency tests in section 6 are the same in substance: 182 days in India makes you resident; for visiting citizens and persons of Indian origin with Indian income above ₹15 lakh, 120 days plus 365 days over the previous four years does too (as RNOR); and an Indian citizen with Indian income above ₹15 lakh who isn't taxable anywhere else is deemed resident. Check your status.

4. TDS: the silent cash trap

Indian payers withhold tax on most income paid to NRIs at higher rates than for residents — on NRO interest, rent and, most painfully, property sales, where the buyer withholds on the full price. You can claim excess back in your return, or reduce it upfront with a lower-deduction certificate. See the property-sale TDS.

5. Use the tax treaty

India has tax treaties (DTAAs) with most countries where NRIs live. To claim treaty rates you generally need a Tax Residency Certificate from your country of residence and the prescribed self-declaration. Treaty relief can stop the same income being taxed twice.

6. Investing in India from abroad

NRIs can invest in Indian shares and mutual funds through NRE (repatriable) or NRO (non-repatriable) routes. Several fund houses restrict investors living in the US or Canada because of reporting rules, so check before you apply. Keep PAN, KYC and your residential status updated with every broker and fund house.

Scam alert: nobody legitimate asks you to pay “RBI clearance fees” or “income tax release charges” to receive money in India. Hang up and verify independently.
